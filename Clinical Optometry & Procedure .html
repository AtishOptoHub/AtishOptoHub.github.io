1. HIRSCHBERG TEST

Definition
  • Hirschberg test is an objective clinical method used to estimate the angle of ocular deviation by observing the position of corneal light reflex (Purkinje image I) in each eye when a light is shone from a fixed distance.
Principle

When a light is projected into the eyes:

  • The corneal reflex should be centrally located in both eyes in normal alignment.
  • In strabismus, reflex shifts away from center in the deviating eye.
  • The amount of displacement correlates with angle of deviation (in prism diopters).
Procedure

Patient Position

  • Seated comfortably
  • Fixating on light source

Examiner Position

  • At 33 cm (near) or ~1 meter (depending on protocol)
  • Uses pen torch / fixation light

Steps

  • Ask patient to fixate on light.
  • Shine light directly at eyes.
  • Observe position of corneal reflex in both eyes.
  • Compare reflex symmetry.
  • Note displacement from pupillary center.
Normal Finding

Corneal light reflex is slightly nasal to pupil center (~0.5 mm) in both eyes symmetrically.

2. KRIMSKY TEST

Definition
  • The Krimsky test is a modification of the Hirschberg test that uses prisms to quantify the angle of ocular deviation by re-centering the displaced corneal light reflex.
Principle
  • Prisms bend light toward their base and displace the image toward the apex.
  • By placing a prism in front of the fixating or deviating eye, the light beam is optically shifted until the corneal reflex in the deviating eye is centered.
  • The prism power required to center the reflex equals the angle of deviation.
Procedure

Patient Position

  • Seated comfortably, looking straight ahead at a light source (penlight at 33 cm or 1 m).

Examiner Position

  • Positioned directly in front of the patient at eye level.

Steps

  • Perform the basic Hirschberg test first to identify the deviating eye and estimate deviation.
  • Place a prism (prism bar or loose prism) in front of one eye (usually the fixating eye or deviating eye).
  • Base orientation rule:
    • Base-Out for Esotropia
    • Base-In for Exotropia
    • Base-Down for Hypertropia
    • Base-Up for Hypotropia
  • Increase prism strength gradually until the corneal reflex in the deviating eye moves to the normal centered position.
  • Record the minimum prism diopter (PD) strength that achieves symmetry.
Normal Finding

No prism is required; corneal light reflexes are symmetric in both eyes naturally.

3. BRUCKNER TEST

Definition
  • The Brückner test is a rapid screening method that uses a direct ophthalmoscope to compare the red reflexes of both eyes simultaneously to detect strabismus, anisometropia, and media opacities.
Principle
  • When light from an ophthalmoscope illuminates both pupils simultaneously, light reflects off the fundus, producing a red reflex.
  • In normal, fully aligned, and emmetropic eyes, both red reflexes are equal in brightness, size, and color.
  • Any asymmetry in brightness or color indicates a potential ocular abnormality (e.g., strabismus or refractive error).
Procedure

Environment Setup

  • Dim the room lights to allow pupil dilation.

Examiner Position

  • Stand approximately 0.5 to 1 meter away from the patient.
  • Look through a direct ophthalmoscope set at "0" or the patient's distance prescription.

Steps

  • Direct the light beam to illuminate both of the patient's eyes simultaneously.
  • Instruct the patient to look directly into the ophthalmoscope light.
  • Observe and compare the red reflex from both pupils at the same time.
  • Evaluate for differences in brightness, color, clarity, or shape of the reflex.
Normal Finding

Both pupils display an equal, bright, and clear red reflex symmetrically.
