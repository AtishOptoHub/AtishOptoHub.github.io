1. HIRSCHBERG TEST

Definition
  • Hirschberg test is an objective clinical method used to estimate the angle of ocular deviation by observing the position of corneal light reflex (Purkinje image I) in each eye when a light is shone from a fixed distance.
Principle
  • When light is projected into the eyes, the corneal reflex is centrally located in both eyes during normal alignment.
  • In strabismus, the reflex shifts away from the center in the deviating eye.
  • Every 1 mm displacement of the corneal light reflex corresponds to approximately 7 to 8 degrees (or 15 to 20 Prism Diopters) of ocular deviation.
Procedure

Patient Position

  • Seated comfortably facing the examiner.
  • Fixating directly on the central light source.

Examiner Position

  • Positioned directly in front at 33 cm (near evaluation) or 1 meter distance.
  • Holds pen torch or light source directly along the line of sight.

Steps

  • Ask patient to fixate steadily on the light source.
  • Shine light directly into both eyes simultaneously.
  • Observe and compare position of corneal light reflex relative to the pupil margin in both eyes.
  • Calculate displacement:
    • At pupil margin (~1.5 mm displacement) = ~15° / ~30 PD deviation.
    • Midway between pupil & limbus (~3 mm displacement) = ~30° / ~60 PD deviation.
    • At limbus (~4.5 mm displacement) = ~45° / ~90 PD deviation.
Normal Finding

Corneal light reflex is symmetrical and slightly nasal to pupil center (~0.5 mm) in both eyes due to normal Angle Alpha.

2. KRIMSKY TEST

Definition
  • The Krimsky test is a quantitative modification of the Hirschberg test that utilizes prisms to measure the exact angle of ocular deviation by neutralizing displaced corneal light reflex.
Principle
  • Prisms bend light rays toward their base, shifting the perceived image toward the apex.
  • By placing a prism in front of either the fixating or deviating eye, the light beam is optically redirected until the corneal light reflex in the deviating eye centers symmetrically.
  • The value of prism power (in Prism Diopters) required to center the reflex represents the magnitude of strabismus.
Procedure

Patient Position

  • Seated comfortably, fixating on a point light source (penlight at 33 cm or 1 m).

Examiner Position

  • Seated directly in front of the patient at eye level.

Steps

  • Perform baseline Hirschberg test to identify deviating eye and orientation of deviation.
  • Place prism bar or loose prism over the fixating eye (Modified Krimsky) or deviating eye (Standard Krimsky).
  • Orient base according to deviation type:
    • Base-Out (BO) for Esotropia
    • Base-In (BI) for Exotropia
    • Base-Down (BD) for Hypertropia
    • Base-Up (BU) for Hypotropia
  • Increase prism power incrementally until corneal light reflex centers symmetrically in both eyes.
  • Record final prism value as deviation measurement.
Normal Finding

0 Prism Diopters (No prism required; reflex is naturally symmetric).

3. BRUCKNER TEST

Definition
  • The Brückner test is a transillumination screening procedure using a direct ophthalmoscope to compare red fundus reflexes simultaneously in both eyes to identify strabismus, anisometropia, media opacities, or posterior pole abnormalities.
Principle
  • Coaxial illumination from direct ophthalmoscope reflects off the fundus through the pupil, producing red reflex in both eyes.
  • In normal binocular alignment and emmetropia, light distribution is equal, resulting in identical red reflex intensity and color in both pupils.
  • In strabismus, light off the deviating eye strikes extrafoveal retina (which absorbs less light), rendering the reflex in the deviating eye significantly brighter and lighter compared to fixating eye.
Procedure

Environment Setup

  • Perform in a dark room to maximize pupil dilation.

Examiner Position

  • Stand 0.5 to 1 meter away from patient.
  • Look through direct ophthalmoscope dial set to "0" or patient's refractive correction.

Steps

  • Direct large coaxial light beam to illuminate both eyes simultaneously.
  • Instruct patient to fixate directly on the ophthalmoscope light.
  • Observe and compare both red reflexes simultaneously for symmetry in brightness, color shade, clarity, and opacities.
Normal Finding

Both pupils demonstrate an equal, clear, symmetric red fundus reflex.

4. COVER UNCOVER TEST

Definition

  • Cover test is an objective clinical test of ocular alignment used to detect, differentiate and measure heterotropia (manifest deviation) and heterophoria (latent deviation).
  • It is considered the gold standard test for evaluating ocular misalignment (strabismus).
Figure 4.1: Ocular Alignment Assessment via Dissociation with Occluder

AIMS / PURPOSES OF COVER TEST

  • Detect presence of ocular deviation.
  • Differentiate heterophoria from heterotropia.
  • Determine direction of deviation.
  • Determine laterality (unilateral or alternating).
  • Assess fixation pattern and control of deviation.
  • Quantify angle of deviation when combined with prism.

CLASSIFICATION OF COVER TEST

1. Cover Test (Single Cover Test)

Used mainly for detection of manifest deviation (tropia).

2. Cover-Uncover Test (CUT)

Used for detection of heterotropia and heterophoria, and differentiating between them.

3. Alternate Cover Test (ACT)

Used for complete dissociation of fusion to measure total deviation (tropia + phoria).
