Ocular Disease - II (Posterior Segment)

Vitreous Body & Vitreous Diseases

Complete Lecture Notes with Anatomic Diagrams

1. Anatomy & Properties of Vitreous

  • General Structure:
    • Transparent Gel filling the vitreous cavity.
    • Makes up to 80% volume of the eye.
    • Provides support to the delicate inner structures of the eye.
    • Provides the pathway for nutrients utilized by the lens, ciliary body, and retina.
  • Composition:
    • Water: ~99%
    • Delicate network of collagen fibrils attached to the Internal Limiting Membrane (ILM) of the retina.
    • Hyaluronic acid, hyalocytes, and mucopolysaccharides.
  • Properties:
    • Hydrophilic gel.
    • Gets readily liquefied as a result of age changes and trauma.

2. Vitreous Attachments & Detachment Types

Vitreous Attachments

  • Posterior lens surface (Weigert ligament)
  • Vitreous base (Ora Serrata) — Strongest attachment
  • Optic disc
  • Paramacular area
  • Paravascular areas

Types of Vitreous Detachment

  • Posterior
  • Basal
  • Anterior / Central

3. Posterior Vitreous Detachment (PVD)

Definition: Separation of the cortical vitreous from retina anywhere posterior to the vitreous base (3-4 mm wide area of attachment at ora serrata).
  • Etiology & Characteristics:
    • A Senile phenomenon occurring with vitreous liquefaction.
    • A hole develops in the posterior hyaloid membrane.
    • More common among aphakics and myopes.
    • Causes: Senile, Myopia, Post-inflammatory, Post-vitreous hemorrhage, Diabetes Mellitus.
  • Clinical Features & Symptoms:
    • Flashes of light and sudden floaters.
    • Collapsed vitreous behind the lens.
    • Optically clear space between detached posterior hyaloid phase and retina.
    • Weiss Ring or Fuchs Ring: Pathognomonic sign.
  • Complications: Retinal breaks, Vitreous hemorrhage, Retinal hemorrhage, Cystoid maculopathy.
  • Diagnosis & Treatment:
    • Examine within 48–72 hours of symptom onset (highest tear risk within 6 weeks).
    • Re-examine 6 weeks after initial onset (tears may be asymptomatic).
    • Treatment: Conservative management, bed rest + head elevation, treat underlying cause, or laser photocoagulation.

4. Vitreous Hemorrhage (VH)

Definition: Leakage of blood into the areas in and around the vitreous humor of the eye, usually from retinal vessels (may present as preretinal or intragel hemorrhage).

Causes

  • Retinal tear, PVD, and Retinal Detachment (RD).
  • Trauma to eye.
  • Inflammatory diseases.
  • Vascular disorders (e.g., Hypertensive Retinopathy).
  • Metabolic diseases (e.g., Diabetic Retinopathy).
  • Bleeding disorders (e.g., Purpura, Hemophilia).

Symptoms & Signs

  • Symptoms: Sudden painless loss of vision, floaters of sudden onset (small VH), red hue/shadows, visual field scotomas, photopsia.
  • Signs: Red fundus reflex may be absent, no view to fundus, red blood cells seen in anterior vitreous.
  • Chronic VH: Yellow-ochre appearance from Hb breakdown.

Diagnosis

  • History taking.
  • Indirect ophthalmoscopy under full pupillary dilation.
  • USG B-Scan.

5. Vitrectomy

Definition: Surgical removal of the vitreous.

Types

  • Anterior Vitrectomy: Removal of anterior part of vitreous.
  • Core Vitrectomy: Removal of central bulk (indicated in endophthalmitis).
  • Subtotal and Total Vitrectomy: Almost whole vitreous is removed.

Techniques & Indications

  • Open-Sky Vitrectomy:
    • Limbal area approach for anterior vitrectomy only.
    • Indications: Vitreous loss during cataract extraction, aphakic keratoplasty, AC reconstruction post-trauma, removal of subluxated/anteriorly dislocated lens.
  • Closed Vitrectomy (Pars Plana Vitrectomy - PPV):
    • Pars plana approach for anterior, core, subtotal, and total vitrectomy.
    • Indications: Endophthalmitis with vitreous loss / abscess, vitreous hemorrhage, removal of intraocular foreign bodies, removal of dropped nucleus or IOL from vitreous cavity.

6. Vitreous Opacities

Vitreous is a transparent structure; any non-transparent structure in it forms an opacity causing symptoms of FLOATERS.

  • Muscae Volitantes: Physiological opacities from primitive hyaloid vasculature residues; fine dots/filaments drifting against bright backgrounds.
  • Persistent Hyperplastic Primary Vitreous (PHPV):
    • Failure of primary vitreous to regress + hypoplasia of posterior vascular network.
    • White pupillary reflex (leukocoria) seen after birth.
    • Differentials: Retinoblastoma, Congenital Cataract, ROP. (CT scan helps in diagnosis).
    • Treatment: Pars plana lensectomy, membrane excision with anterior vitrectomy. Visual prognosis is poor.
  • Inflammatory Opacities: Exudates poured into vitreous in Anterior Uveitis, Posterior Uveitis, Pars Planitis, Panuveitis, Endophthalmitis.
  • Vitreous Aggregates & Condensation: Commonest cause of opacities (condensation of collagen fibrils; senile, myopic, post-traumatic, post-inflammatory).
  • Asteroid Hyalosis: Small white rounded bodies suspended in vitreous gel (calcium-containing lipids); unilateral, asymptomatic in old patients with healthy vitreous.
  • Synchysis Scintillans: "Shower of golden rain" on ophthalmoscopy (cholesterol crystals); symptomless and untreatable.
  • Red & Tumor Cell Opacities: Caused by small VH or floating tumor cells in Retinoblastoma / Reticulum cell sarcoma.
